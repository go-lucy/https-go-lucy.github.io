01 / The provocation

We built an entire industry around the fact that the people who needed software couldn't build it. That constraint is disappearing. Why are we still running the same machine?

02 / The principles

Four letters. A different operating model.

L

Live by default.

Needs emerge in real time. Software should be capable of responding in real time, with safeguards appropriate to the change.

U

Users are creators.

The people closest to a problem should have the power to help shape its solution—not just submit a ticket.

C

Continuous creation.

Stop treating sprints and releases as the organizing unit of work. Let products evolve continuously.

Y

Your intent leads.

Humans define what matters and remain accountable. Intelligent agents help turn intent into reality.

03 / The Go Lucy Manifesto · v1.0

LONG LIVE
CREATION.

For fifty years, we've organized software development around one fundamental constraint:

The people who needed software couldn't build it.

So we invented an entire industry to bridge the gap.

Business analysts to translate needs. Product managers to prioritize them. Designers to visualize them. Engineers to implement them. QA teams to test them. Project managers to coordinate everyone.

We invented requirements documents, tickets, backlogs, sprints, standups, roadmaps, release trains, and feature flags.

We called it progress.

And for a long time, it was.

But the constraint that justified this machinery is disappearing.

Software no longer needs to pass through a factory of specialists before it can exist.

That changes everything.

The end of the software factory

Agile was a revolution for its time.

It made software development more responsive, more collaborative, and less wasteful than the rigid processes that preceded it.

But Agile still assumes software is expensive to produce. That engineering capacity is scarce. That work must be broken into tickets, assigned to teams, and delivered in increments.

AI doesn't just make that process faster.

It challenges the reason the process exists.

Yet much of the industry is responding by putting AI inside the old machinery.

AI writes the tickets. AI estimates the stories. AI generates the code. AI summarizes the standups.

We're automating a factory that no longer needs to exist.

This isn't transformation. It's preservation.

The customer is no longer waiting

Imagine a customer using a piece of software.

Something is confusing. Something is missing. Something doesn't work the way they think it should.

Today, they might submit feedback. A product manager might interpret it. A team might prioritize it. An engineer might build it. Months later, the customer might see a change.

Or nothing happens.

Now imagine the customer circles the confusing thing on their screen and says:

“This should work differently.”

An agent understands the context, proposes a change, builds it, tests it, and shows the customer what the new experience would look like.

The customer responds.

The software evolves.

Not every change should ship automatically. Security, reliability, shared architecture, and the needs of other users still matter.

But the customer no longer has to wait for an organization to translate their intent into a backlog.

The distance between wanting and having collapses.

Software becomes a living thing

We have treated software as something we build, launch, and maintain.

A product is conceived. A roadmap is approved. Features are developed. Releases are shipped.

Then the cycle repeats.

But what if software were never finished?

What if every product could continuously respond to the people using it?

What if the interface adapted to different users? What if workflows changed as needs changed? What if a company's software evolved as quickly as the company itself?

The future isn't a faster release cycle.

It's the disappearance of the release cycle as the organizing principle of software creation.

Software becomes continuously adaptable, with changes evaluated and governed according to their risk.

IMAGINE.

CEOs: Stop asking your technology team what's possible this quarter. Start asking what's possible today.

PRODUCT MANAGERS: Kill the backlog grooming. Kill the priority negotiations. Stop managing what might get built. Start shaping what's being created.

ENGINEERS: No more translating vague tickets into code nobody wants. Build the intelligence, architecture, and guardrails that make continuous creation possible.

DESIGNERS: No more throwing designs over the wall. Your ideas come alive while you're still imagining them.

CUSTOMERS: No more “We'll add it to the roadmap.” Point to what you want changed. Watch it change.

EVERYONE: Less waiting. Less translating. Less coordinating. More creating.

Imagine what you could create if you got all that time, energy, and intelligence back.

No backlog standing between an idea and an experiment.

No release train holding finished improvements hostage.

No translation chain separating the people who need software from the people who create it.

No factory.

We call this going Full Lucy.

Everyone creates. Everything evolves.

This doesn't mean everyone becomes an engineer.

It means people no longer need to become engineers to participate directly in creation.

The people closest to the problem can shape the solution.

A nurse can improve a clinical workflow. A teacher can redesign a learning experience. A customer can fix a frustrating interaction. A founder can test an idea without waiting for a development team.

And the role of technical specialists becomes more important in a different way.

Engineers build the systems that make this possible. They establish boundaries, reliability, security, interoperability, and trust.

Designers shape experiences, coherence, and taste.

Product leaders discover opportunities, reconcile competing needs, and decide what should exist.

The work shifts from managing the production of software to directing the evolution of systems.

That's not a minor job description change.

It's an industry-wide reallocation of human intelligence.

Four principles for what comes next

L — Live by default. Software is not a finished artifact. It is a living system that can continually improve.

U — Users are creators. The people experiencing the problem should have a direct hand in shaping the solution.

C — Continuous creation. Replace long translation and delivery chains with rapid cycles of intent, creation, evaluation, and learning.

Y — Your intent leads. Humans decide what matters. Agents help turn that intent into reality, within clear boundaries of trust and accountability.

This is not a better Agile

We're not proposing another framework for managing tickets.

We're not proposing a more efficient sprint.

We're not proposing that every company replace its engineers with agents.

We're proposing that the fundamental unit of software creation changes.

From the ticket to the intent.

From the roadmap to the evolving product.

From handoffs to direct collaboration.

From software delivery to software evolution.

And from users as recipients to users as participants.

The old processes won't disappear overnight. Some systems will demand rigorous, deliberate release controls. Some industries will move more slowly than others.

But those constraints should determine where we need safeguards, not force us to organize everything around yesterday's limitations.

The invitation

To the founders and CEOs tired of asking why everything takes so long.

To the engineers who want to engineer, not administer tickets.

To the product people who want to create, not coordinate.

To the designers who want to see their ideas come alive.

To the customers who've waited long enough.

Let's build something better.

Go Lucy is an invitation to rethink how software gets created.

Not merely to predict the future, but to build it.

To experiment with new workflows. To challenge inherited assumptions. To develop the tools, practices, and principles that make continuous creation possible.

We don't claim to have every answer.

But we believe the question has changed.

It's no longer:

How do we develop software faster?

It's:

What becomes possible when software development is no longer the bottleneck?

Stop managing the backlog.

Start creating the future.

GO LUCY.
