01 / The provocation

We built an entire industry around the fact that the people who needed software couldn't build it. That constraint is disappearing. Why are we still running the same machine?

02 / The principles

Four letters. A different operating model.

L

Live by default.

Needs emerge in real time. Software should be capable of responding in real time, with safeguards appropriate to the change.

U

Users are creators.

The people closest to a problem should have the power to help shape its solution—not just submit a ticket.

C

Continuous creation.

Stop treating sprints and releases as the organizing unit of work. Let products evolve continuously.

Y

Your intent leads.

Humans define what matters and remain accountable. Intelligent agents help turn intent into reality.

03 / The Go Lucy Manifesto · v1.0

LONG LIVE
CREATION.

For fifty years, we've organized software development around one fundamental constraint:

The people who needed software couldn't build it.

So we invented an industry to bridge the gap. Analysts to translate needs. Product managers to prioritize them. Designers to visualize them. Engineers to implement them. Testers to verify them. Managers to coordinate everyone.

We invented requirements documents, tickets, backlogs, sprints, standups, roadmaps, release trains, and feature flags. We called it progress. And for a long time, it was.

But the constraint that justified this machinery is disappearing.

The end of the software factory.

Agile was a revolution for its time. But it still assumes software is expensive to produce, engineering capacity is scarce, and work must be broken into tickets and delivered in increments.

AI doesn't just make that process faster. It challenges the reason the process exists.

The industry is responding by making the old process faster. AI writes the tickets. AI estimates the stories. AI generates the code. AI summarizes the standups.

We're automating a factory that no longer needs to exist.

This isn't transformation. It's preservation.

The customer is no longer waiting.

Something in your software is confusing. Something is missing. Something should work differently.

Today, you submit feedback. Someone interprets it. Someone prioritizes it. Someone builds it. Months later, maybe something changes.

Now imagine circling the problem on your screen and saying, “This should work differently.” An agent understands the context, proposes a change, builds it, tests it, and shows you the result. You respond. The software evolves.

Not every change should ship automatically. Security, reliability, and the needs of other users still matter. But the customer no longer has to wait for an organization to translate intent into a backlog.

The distance between wanting and having collapses.

Software becomes a living thing.

We have treated software as something we build, launch, and maintain. What if it were never finished? What if every product could respond continuously to the people using it, with changes governed by their risk?

The future isn't a faster release cycle. It's the disappearance of the release cycle as the organizing principle of software creation.

IMAGINE.

CEOs: Stop asking your technology team what's possible this quarter. Start asking what's possible today.

PRODUCT MANAGERS: Kill the backlog grooming. Kill the priority negotiations. Stop managing what might get built. Start shaping what's being created.

ENGINEERS: No more translating vague tickets into code nobody wants. Build the intelligence, architecture, and guardrails that make continuous creation possible.

DESIGNERS: No more throwing designs over the wall. Your ideas come alive while you're still imagining them.

CUSTOMERS: No more “We'll add it to the roadmap.” Point to what you want changed. Watch it change.

EVERYONE: Less waiting. Less translating. Less coordinating. More creating.

No backlog standing between an idea and an experiment. No release train holding finished improvements hostage. No translation chain separating the people who need software from the people who create it.

No factory.
We call this going Full Lucy.

Everyone creates. Everything evolves.

This doesn't mean everyone becomes an engineer. It means people no longer need to become engineers to participate directly in creation.

Engineers make systems reliable, secure, and trustworthy. Designers bring coherence and taste. Product leaders discover opportunities and reconcile competing needs. Domain experts and customers shape what works.

Human intelligence shifts from coordinating the production of software to directing the evolution of systems.

Four principles. One new operating model.

L — Live by default. Software is a living system, capable of continual improvement.

U — Users are creators. The people experiencing a problem can directly shape its solution.

C — Continuous creation. Intent, creation, evaluation, and learning replace long chains of handoffs.

Y — Your intent leads. Humans determine what matters. Agents help realize it within clear boundaries of trust and accountability.

This is not a better Agile.

We're not proposing a more efficient sprint or another framework for managing tickets. We're proposing that the fundamental unit of software creation changes: from tickets to intent, from scheduled delivery to governed evolution, from users as recipients to users as participants.

Some systems will always demand deliberate controls. Those safeguards should be designed for the actual risk—not inherited from yesterday's production constraints.

The invitation.

To CEOs who are tired of watching opportunity wait in a queue. To engineers who want to engineer, not administer tickets. To product people who want to create, not coordinate. To designers who want to see ideas come alive. To customers who've waited long enough.

Go Lucy is an open invitation to challenge inherited assumptions, experiment with new ways of working, and build the tools that make continuous creation possible.

It's no longer a question of how to develop software faster. It's what becomes possible when software development is no longer the bottleneck.

Stop managing the backlog.
Start creating the future.
GO LUCY.
